# Research Question Analysis

Answers below are drawn only from files already generated by this project's
experiments (paths cited inline) and from `docs/literature_review.md` /
`results/literature_matrix.csv`. Where evidence is genuinely mixed or
inconclusive, that is stated plainly rather than resolved by choosing a more
convenient reading (master prompt section 49).

## Study design note relevant to all four RQs

The 5 repeated seeds {42, 123, 2026, 7, 99} each drive a **fresh 80/20
stratified train/test split** (`src/preprocessing.py:train_test_split_stratified`
passes `seed` straight into `train_test_split`'s `random_state`), not the
same split re-run with different downstream randomness. So "multi-seed"
variance here reflects the full pipeline's sensitivity to which 60 rows land
in the test set, plus masking draws and RF training randomness — a broader
and more honest source of variance than reseeding RF alone, but it also
means the n=5 seeds are the only thing standing between a point estimate and
noise. `results/metrics/repeated_seeds_e0.csv` shows this variance is large:
the complete-data RF baseline alone swings from F1=0.667 (seed 42) to
F1=0.857 (seed 123), std=0.081. Every RQ answer below is read against that
backdrop.

## RQ1 — How does missing data affect healthcare prediction performance?

**Missingness measurably hurts prediction, and this is the one claim in this
project with actual statistical support** (`docs/statistical_analysis.md`,
"Cost of missingness" section; full numbers in
`results/tables/statistical_tests.csv`). Comparing the complete-data E0
baseline (5-seed mean F1 = 0.776, `results/metrics/repeated_seeds_e0.csv`)
against each mechanism's best-performing imputer at each level
(`results/metrics/e1_e2_e3_multiseed_summary.csv`), a paired t-test across
the 5 seeds reached p<0.05 in **4 of 9** mechanism×level cells (MCAR 10%
p=0.017, MCAR 20% p=0.012, MNAR 20% p=0.008, MNAR 30% p=0.024), with two more
borderline (MAR 20% p=0.065, MCAR 30% p=0.050). Every one of the 9 cells
shows complete-data performance above imputed performance in direction, even
where not individually significant — no cell trended the other way.

What the evidence does **not** support: a claim that one mechanism is
"easier" than another. Direct MCAR-vs-MAR-vs-MNAR paired tests at matched
levels found **0 of 9 significant** (smallest p=0.088, MCAR vs MNAR at 20%,
`results/tables/statistical_tests.csv`). The single-seed impression from
earlier in the project — that MAR/MNAR looked better than MCAR — was
investigated as a possible column-coverage confound, fixed
(`docs/experiment_log.md`), and even after fixing it, the residual gap did
not survive multi-seed testing. **Conclusion: missing data hurts prediction
regardless of mechanism; this dataset's 60-row test set and 5 seeds cannot
distinguish which mechanism hurts more.** This matches an independent
finding in Shadbahr et al. (2023) that missingness *rate* (not mechanism or
imputer) drives most of the downstream performance variance
(`docs/literature_review.md` §1).

## RQ2 — Which selected imputation method performs best for this dataset?

**No imputer is statistically distinguishable from any other at this sample
size.** The best-vs-second-best imputer comparison within each of the 9
mechanism×level cells found **0 of 9 significant** (all p≥0.42, most p>0.7;
`results/tables/statistical_tests.csv`). Point-estimate rankings do exist in
`results/metrics/e1_e2_e3_multiseed_summary.csv` — e.g. at MCAR 10%, KNN
edges out the others on mean F1 (0.722 vs. 0.700–0.716) — but given the
per-cell F1 standard deviations (0.03–0.17 across seeds), these rankings
should be read as **directional/suggestive, not conclusive**. Aracri et al.
(2025) reported the same pattern on a comparable clinical classification
task: no imputer uniformly best across classifiers
(`docs/literature_review.md` §2).

What would resolve this: more seeds (the standard error of a 5-seed mean is
large relative to the observed inter-imputer gaps), a larger dataset (this
one has only 60 held-out test rows per split), or a within-seed
cross-validated comparison design with more folds. None of these were
pursued here due to compute budget (documented in
`docs/statistical_analysis.md` limitations).

## RQ3 — Can GA-based feature selection improve prediction, or preserve performance with fewer features?

The honest answer is **mixed, and the apparent single-seed wins did not
replicate**, but GA does deliver one clear, reproducible benefit:

- **Complete data, seed 42** (`results/metrics/all_features_vs_ga_complete.csv`):
  GA reduced the feature set from 12 to 4 (66.7% reduction) and *improved*
  F1 (0.667→0.706) and accuracy (0.817→0.833), but *reduced* ROC-AUC
  (0.883→0.815). Not a clean win on every metric.
- **Imputed data, seed 42, all 9 E6 cells** (`results/tables/four_way_comparison.csv`):
  GA improved F1 in 3/9 cells (MCAR 20%, MCAR 30%, MNAR 10%), tied in 1/9
  (MAR 20%), and reduced F1 in 5/9 (MAR 10%, MAR 30%, MCAR 10%, MNAR 20%,
  MNAR 30%). No consistent direction.
- **Multi-seed replication check, 3 representative cells × 3 seeds**
  (`results/metrics/e6_multiseed_subset.csv`): recomputing the mean
  all-features vs. GA F1 across seeds {42, 123, 2026} for the cell that
  looked like GA's biggest single-seed win (MCAR 30%, iterative imputer):
  mean all-features F1 = 0.647 vs. mean GA F1 = 0.632 — **GA's apparent
  advantage inverts once averaged across seeds.** The same pattern holds for
  the other two sampled cells (MNAR 30%: 0.634 vs. 0.607; MAR 20%: 0.724 vs.
  0.676) — GA underperforms all-features on average in all 3 cells checked,
  confirming the single-seed "wins" recorded above were largely noise.

The one finding that **does** hold up and is not fabricated or
cherry-picked: GA **reliably and reproducibly selects a small, stable,
clinically plausible feature subset**. Across 5 independent GA runs on
complete data (`results/tables/ga_feature_frequency.csv`), `time` and
`ejection_fraction` were selected in 5/5 runs, `serum_creatinine` in 4/5, and
`age` in 0/5. This matches the dataset's origin paper's own finding
(Chicco & Jurman 2020) that ejection fraction and serum creatinine alone
predict survival comparably to the full feature set
(`docs/literature_review.md` §3) — an external validation the GA had no way
to have seen in advance. **Conclusion: on this dataset, GA is a reliable
tool for finding a defensible reduced feature subset, but not a reliable
tool for improving Random Forest's predictive performance beyond what all
12 features already achieve.** A plausible explanation (Pudjihartono et al.
2022; general wrapper-method literature) is that a 12-feature space and
239-row training set give a genetic wrapper search limited room to find a
genuinely superior subset and more room to overfit its own CV-based fitness
signal — this is offered as a plausible explanation, not a proven mechanism.

## RQ4 — How does Imputation + GA + Random Forest compare with selected baselines and existing research?

Own-results comparison, per `results/tables/four_way_comparison.csv` and
`results/metrics/repeated_seeds_e0.csv` (all four arms on the same dataset,
same splits — this is a valid internal comparison, unlike anything below
against literature):

| Arm | Feature count | Representative F1 |
|---|---|---|
| Complete + RF (E0) | 12 | 0.776 ± 0.081 (5-seed mean) |
| Imputed + RF (E1–E3, best imputer/cell) | 12 | 0.615–0.742 across 9 cells (`e1_e2_e3_multiseed_summary.csv`) |
| Complete + GA + RF (E5) | 4 | 0.706 (seed 42; single-seed) |
| Imputed + GA + RF (E6) | 2–11 (varies by cell) | 0.417–0.756 across 9 cells (`e6_imputed_ga_rf.csv` / `four_way_comparison.csv`) |

Ordering by point estimate: complete-data RF is highest, imputed-RF is
lower (consistent with RQ1's statistically supported missingness cost), and
adding GA on top of either complete or imputed data does not produce a
consistent further improvement — in most cells it is roughly comparable to
or slightly below the non-GA counterpart, per RQ3.

**Comparison with literature** (`docs/literature_review.md`,
`results/literature_matrix.csv`) is reported as *contextual*, not
head-to-head, since every cited study used a different dataset and/or
split — per master prompt section 36, this project does not present
cross-dataset numbers as a direct comparison:

- **Agrees**: Shadbahr et al. (2023) — missingness rate matters more than
  imputer choice; Ren et al. (2024) and Aracri et al. (2025) — no single
  imputer dominates across contexts; Chicco & Jurman (2020) — ejection
  fraction and serum creatinine are the dominant predictive features on this
  exact dataset, independently reconfirmed by this project's GA.
- **Diverges**: Kumar & Sahoo (2017) reported GA+RF reaching their highest
  accuracy (93.2%) on a cardiovascular dataset — a case where GA clearly
  helped. This project's GA did not reliably outperform all-features RF once
  tested across multiple seeds. Both results are reported as-is; the
  divergence is treated as a genuine, dataset/scale-dependent finding, not
  evidence that either study is wrong.

## Limitations affecting interpretation of all four RQs

- **Single primary dataset, 299 rows** (239 train / 60 test) — small by
  general ML standards, and the master prompt's scope-control (section 2)
  intentionally restricts this RP to one primary dataset.
- **n=5 seeds** is statistically thin for paired testing; most comparisons
  (18 of 27 in `results/tables/statistical_tests.csv`) are non-significant,
  which is evidence of "not distinguishable from noise at this sample size,"
  not evidence of "no real effect" (`docs/statistical_analysis.md`
  Limitations).
- **Seeds reseed the full pipeline including the train/test split** (see
  design note above), not just model training — a more thorough but also
  higher-variance multi-seed design than reseeding RF alone.
- **No multiple-comparison correction** was applied to the 27 statistical
  tests (documented rationale in `docs/statistical_analysis.md`): with n=5,
  any standard correction would make it nearly impossible for any comparison
  to reach significance regardless of true effect size. Individual p-values
  should be read as exploratory/suggestive.
- **GA fitness evaluation used 50 trees instead of the final 200-tree RF
  configuration** (`src/config.py:GA_FITNESS_N_ESTIMATORS`), a documented
  ~5x cost reduction; final reported all-features-vs-GA comparisons use the
  full 200-tree configuration on both sides, so reported performance numbers
  are unaffected, but the GA's internal search itself saw a noisier fitness
  signal than the final evaluation.
- **E6's multi-seed check covered only 3 of 9 cells at 3 seeds** (not the
  full 9×5 grid) due to compute budget — sufficient to show the single-seed
  GA pattern does not replicate, but not exhaustive.
